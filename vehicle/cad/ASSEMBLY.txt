RC CAR — STATIC VISUALIZATION ASSEMBLY

Open full_assembly.FCStd directly in FreeCAD. It contains 74 mechanical geometry
instances, organized into front, middle and rear App::Part containers. The
Fairing group additionally embeds the printable body from fairing.FCStd. See
fairing/FAIRING.txt and its manifest for the current body-part count. Each
component has an editable Placement and records its source body in Reference.
No assembly workbench, joints, solver, Python features or external files are
required to open it. car.FCStd and the two older assemblies are unchanged.

Use this model as the packaging reference for the installed fairing and future
body changes. Revision 3 separates hood/deck, fenders and cabin panels for lower
support use. Reuse the already printed Rear_wing and Front_bumper unchanged. The source
parametric design remains car.FCStd; assembly parts are snapshots, not live
links. Do not use the assembled car as a printable fused solid.

COORDINATES AND DIMENSIONS (mm)
X: across the car; +Y: forward; +Z: up.
Origin: front chassis design datum, with its original Body placement removed.
Middle module: translation (0,-121,5).
Rear module: translation (0,-336,0), rotation 180 degrees about Z.
Wheel centers: front Y=-32.5, rear Y=-301; all Z=28.
Wheelbase: 268.5. Wheel envelopes: diameter 89, width 36.
Front track: 282.401; rear track: 282.384 (wheel center to wheel center).
Bare mechanical bounds (fairing excluded), including spindle ends and camera:
  X -161.200 to 161.200; Y -345.622 to 26.131; Z -16.500 to 102.400.
Bare car size: approximately 322.4 wide x 371.8 long x 118.9 high.
Fairing/wing dimensions and print splits are documented in fairing/FAIRING.txt.
Ground plane for the nominal tire envelopes: Z=-16.5.
L/R instance suffixes identify negative/positive global X, respectively.

WHAT IS INCLUDED
Three chassis sections; upper/lower suspension arms and hinge pins; four
knuckles, wheels, wheel spindles and hub bearing pairs; front bearing spacers;
rear outer suspension pins; four two-piece shocks; steering bellcrank pair,
tie rods, servo, servo mounts, horn and servo link; electronics table, upright
ESP32/camera support; motor, pinion, main spur gear,
output bearings, half-shafts, wheel drive cups and dogbones.

Older belt-drive pulleys, the old straight rear axle, standalone generic servo
horn and shaft collars are superseded hardware and are not installed alongside
the current spur-gear drivetrain. Missing commercial electronics, battery,
wiring, springs and general nuts/bolts are not represented by invented parts.
The current SteeringBellcrank body already contains both sides and the bridge.
Chassis locks are deliberately omitted from the assembly.

PLACEMENT AND FIT LIMITS
This is a static visualization pose, not a mechanically verified mechanism.
Chassis mounting axes and equipment-table hole patterns were used for alignment.
Suspension arms were rotated around their inner hinges to meet upright knuckle
ball/pin centers, with level axle heights. Source part dimensions are retained.
The front shock lower eyes attach at the middle of the lower arms, in the
14 mm gap between bracket ears (source point -32,0,-6.7). Rear lower eyes
attach at the aft end of the lower-arm mounting tube (source point -32,35,-7;
the rear module is rotated 180 degrees). Front upper anchors are unchanged.
Rear upper eyes attach behind the same chassis ears: the ears span local
Y=-6.5..-1.5, and each eye center is at Y=2.4, allowing its 3.7 mm half-width
plus 0.2 mm clearance behind the aft face. X/Z pin axes remain +/-30 and 65.
Rear lower attachments are unchanged; shocks lean behind the upper wishbones.
Both shocks have zero overlap with their upper wishbones, with minimum static
clearance of 4.300 mm (upper housing) and 11.032 mm (lower housing).
Front lower arms are turned over and repitched around their chassis pins:
their socket mouths face up onto the lower knuckle studs. Upper arm mouths
face down onto the upper studs. Saved sphere centers are retained.
Tie-rod socket mouths face down onto their studs. The servo link mouths face
inward along +X onto the lateral bellcrank/horn studs, with roll set explicitly.
Servo-support wall holes coincide with all four servo-ear fixing holes and
their mating faces touch. Relative to the first assembly, supports moved
+8.24 mm in Y, -1.8 mm in X and -0.15 mm in Z. These positions follow the servo
fixings; the support feet and chassis-hole pattern have not been redesigned.
The original wheels are simple cylindrical envelopes with through holes.
Steering links and dogbones are rigid, centered between estimated endpoints;
small endpoint gaps remain. Shocks use both saved housing shapes, with an
illustrative telescoping overlap; the source contains no proper internal rod.
The servo horn and camera-support hinge are packaging poses.
Check these against the physical car before designing close-fitting fairing
mounts. Allow for steering and suspension travel: the previews show only the
maximum-compression pose, as confirmed by the user, not normal ride height.
The fairing reserves downward travel plus only 3 mm upward tolerance; see
fairing/FAIRING.txt for the explicit motion assumptions.

The saved SteeringBellcrank fillet has invalid edge tolerances. Only its
assembly snapshot was repaired using Shape.fix(1e-5,1e-6,1e-3). It is valid after
repair, with a volume change of about -0.0000012 mm^3. No source edit was made.

REBUILDING
Close full_assembly.FCStd, then run rebuild_assembly.FCMacro using FreeCAD's
Macro dialog. Keep the macro beside car.FCStd, or set the RC_CAD_DIR environment
variable to this directory. The macro writes full_assembly.FCStd and reinstalls
fairing.FCStd if present. It does not rebuild fairing geometry; run
rebuild_fairing.FCMacro for that. Rebuilding overwrites the generated assembly,
including manual placement changes in it.
The macro deliberately does not recompute or save the source document.
